The Replicator initiative, explained for founders

Updated July 29, 2026 · Free educational guide · verify details at the official sources below

Replicator is a DoD initiative, which DIU helps lead, to field thousands of attritable, all-domain autonomous systems within 18–24 months. Round one targeted roughly $1B across FY2024–25; DoD announced its first Replicator 2 acquisition on January 11, 2026, with a focus on counter-UAS. Startups typically get in through DIU Commercial Solutions Openings, existing vendor pools, or by supplying Blue UAS-cleared platforms and components.

If you build drones, autonomy, or the software that ties them together, Replicator is the DoD program you keep hearing about—and the one most misunderstood by founders. Here's what it is and how you actually get in.

What Replicator is

Announced in 2023, Replicator is a Department of Defense initiative to field thousands of attritable, all-domain autonomous systems within an aggressive 18–24 month window. ‘Attritable’ means cheap enough to lose—the opposite of exquisite, decade-long platforms. The goal is mass, produced fast, from a resilient industrial base. DIU helps lead the effort, using its speed and commercial reach to move systems from selection to fielding.

How it's funded

The first round (Replicator 1) targeted roughly $1 billion across FY2024 and FY2025, drawn from reprogrammed and appropriated funds. It's structured in tranches—successive capability sets rather than one giant buy. On January 11, 2026, DoD announced its first Replicator 2 acquisition, with counter-uncrewed-aircraft (C-UAS) as an early priority.

Where DIU fits

DIU is a primary execution engine. It runs the commercial solicitations, selects vendors, and awards fast prototype Other Transaction agreements that can scale into production. DIU has publicly announced software vendor pools to support Replicator—the connective tissue (autonomy, mission software, data) that lets many cheap systems act as one.

How a startup actually gets in

There is no single ‘apply to Replicator’ button. Realistic paths:

  • DIU Commercial Solutions Openings. Watch DIU's open solicitations for problem statements aligned to Replicator capability sets, and respond with a tight solution brief.
  • Blue UAS. If you make drones or key components, getting onto the Blue UAS Cleared List (now managed by DCMA) makes you procurement-ready for Replicator buyers.
  • Vendor pools and teaming. Many Replicator dollars flow through existing awardees. Subcontracting or supplying a prime already in a tranche is a legitimate on-ramp.
  • Counter-UAS focus. With Replicator 2 leaning into C-UAS, startups in detection, tracking, and defeat have a timely opening.

Why the structure matters to you

Because Replicator moves in tranches, there is no single deadline—there is a rolling series of capability sets, each with its own solicitations, primes and timelines. That changes your strategy from ‘apply once’ to ‘stay positioned.’ The companies that win are usually already procurement-ready when a tranche opens: Blue UAS-cleared, with a manufacturable product and a government relationship in place. Getting cleared or teamed before the buy is announced is often the difference between catching a tranche and missing it.

What DoD is actually buying

Replicator prizes mass and affordability over exquisite performance. A system that is 80% as capable but a fraction of the cost—and can be built in volume from a resilient, trusted supply chain—fits the thesis better than a gold-plated one-off. Software matters just as much as airframes: the autonomy, mission software and data links that let many cheap systems coordinate are core to the initiative, which is why DIU stood up dedicated software vendor pools.

What to keep in mind

Replicator rewards manufacturable, affordable, deliverable-now technology—not science projects. Program timelines are tight and, as public reporting notes, ambitious targets have slipped and budgets shift year to year. Treat it as a fast-moving procurement wave you position for continuously, through DIU's vehicles and the Blue UAS pipeline, rather than a one-time grant to chase. Keep your supply chain clean, your unit economics real, and a DoD sponsor engaged. For authoritative background, read the CRS overview and DIU's Replicator page.
