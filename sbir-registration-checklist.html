From whiteboard to SBIR-ready: the registration checklist.

Updated July 28, 2026 · Free educational guide · verify details at the official sources linked below

Before you can submit an SBIR proposal you need, in order: a formed entity with an EIN, a SAM.gov registration with UEI (allow 2–6 weeks — this is the choke point; CAGE code comes automatically), an SBIR.gov company registry ID (1–2 days), and the agency's own portal (DSIP for DoD, Research.gov for NSF, Grants.gov + eRA Commons for NIH). Start at least eight weeks before your deadline.

The chain, in order (don't parallelize the first two)

  1. Form the entity + get an EIN. Any for-profit form works (LLC is common). Days to two weeks.
  2. SAM.gov. The federal vendor registry. You'll receive your UEI (Unique Entity ID — this replaced DUNS) and, automatically for U.S. entities, a CAGE code. Entity validation is the notorious bottleneck: plan 2–6 weeks, sometimes more. It's free — ignore paid "expediters." Renew annually.
  3. SBIR.gov company registry. Registers your small business with the program itself; issues your SBC control ID. One to two business days.
  4. Agency portal(s): DSIP (dodsbirsttr.mil) for anything DoD including DARPA; Research.gov for NSF; Grants.gov + eRA Commons for NIH (eRA org registration can add two weeks); PAMS for DOE; NASA's submission system for NASA.

Rule of thumb: begin the chain at least eight weeks before your target deadline. More proposals die of late registrations than of bad science.

While you wait: the free help almost nobody uses

  • FAST partners. SBA funds one organization in each state to help founders with SBIR for free — proposal reviews, registration help, and in many states "Phase 0" micro-grants ($2K–$25K) that pay proposal-writing costs. Find yours at sbir.gov/community/fast.
  • State SBIR matches. Fifteen-plus states add non-dilutive money on top of federal wins — Kentucky up to $100K on Phase I, Virginia up to $75K, North Carolina ~50% matches, Massachusetts up to $500K for life-science Phase IIs. Some require applying within a window after the federal award — check your state before you win, not after.
  • Program staff. During DoD pre-release you may talk to the topic author directly; NSF and NIH program officers answer cold emails. Use the window.
If your project needs simulation

The eight weeks you spend waiting on SAM.gov are perfect for building the technical spine of your proposal — including the simulation studies that become your preliminary-data section. Ask us early what eval access could look like, so the tools are ready when the paperwork is.

A realistic first-timer timeline

WeeksWhat's happening
0–2Pick agency + topic (see grants vs contracts); form entity; start SAM.gov; NSF founders send the Project Pitch
2–6SAM validation grinds; draft technical volume; build preliminary evidence (analysis/simulation); collect support letters
6–8SBIR.gov + agency portal registrations; budget; commercialization plan; internal red-team review
8+Submit early — portals crash at deadlines. Then keep building; decisions take 3–10 months depending on agency
