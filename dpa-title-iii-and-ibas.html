DPA Title III & IBAS: industrial-base money

Updated July 29, 2026 · Free educational guide · verify details at the official sources below

DPA Title III (50 U.S.C. §§4531–4534) and the IBAS program invest to close domestic defense industrial-base gaps using grants, purchase commitments, loans, and loan guarantees. Combined DPA and IBAS investment exceeds ~$6.7B since 2015. Many projects are competed through the Defense Industrial Base Consortium (DIBC) via Requests for Project Proposals under an Other Transaction agreement.

Fixing the base, not the product

APFIT buys your product and OSC lends against your factory, but DPA Title III and IBAS answer a different question: is the domestic industrial base capable of producing what national defense needs? When the answer is no — a material only made overseas, a single fragile supplier, a capacity that doesn't exist — these authorities invest to fix it.

DPA Title III (50 U.S.C. §§4531–4534) can deploy grants, purchase commitments, loans, and loan guarantees to scale emerging technologies and sustain critical production. IBAS, a Title 10 authority, focuses on analyzing and sustaining the industrial base — capacity expansion, workforce, and supply-chain resilience. Together they've put more than ~$6.7B to work since 2015.

The consortium is the door

Here's the practical part: much of this money doesn't come from a portal you'd stumble onto. It's competed through the Defense Industrial Base Consortium (DIBC) via Requests for Project Proposals under an Other Transaction agreement. If you're not a consortium member watching the RPPs, you simply won't see the opportunities — including recent ones targeting critical minerals and materials.

Who fits

These programs favor companies that address a documented shortfall the DoD already worries about. If you're building capacity for a critical component, reshoring a material, or eliminating a single-source risk, you're in the target zone. Engage OUSD(A&S) Industrial Base Policy, join the DIBC, and map your capability to a named gap.

Grants, commitments, or guarantees — know which you're getting

One thing to understand up front: DPA Title III isn't a single instrument. The same authority can show up as a grant, a purchase commitment (the government agrees to buy output, de-risking your revenue), a loan, or a loan guarantee. Which one you're offered depends on the shortfall and the economics. A purchase commitment, for example, can be as valuable as cash because it makes your line financeable by private lenders. Read the specific RPP carefully — the mechanism shapes your obligations as much as the dollar figure does.

These are also relationship-heavy programs. The government has to have already concluded there's a gap worth closing before money moves. So part of the work is helping the relevant industrial-base office see and document the shortfall your capability addresses — which means engaging OUSD(A&S) Industrial Base Policy well before an RPP that fits you ever appears.

How it stacks

DPA/IBAS pairs naturally with the rest: prove it with SBIR, sell units through APFIT, finance the plant with OSC loans, and close the base gap with Title III. See the full stack.
