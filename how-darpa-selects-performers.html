How DARPA selects performers

Updated July 29, 2026 · Free educational guide · verify details at the official sources below

DARPA selection is driven by program managers evaluating proposals against a BAA's published criteria — usually scientific/technical merit, relevance to the program's metrics, realism of the plan, and cost. It's a best-value judgment, not a lottery or a formula, and preliminary evidence that your physics works carries real weight.

It starts and ends with the program manager

DARPA program managers are term-limited technical leaders who each own a portfolio and are personally accountable for its success. They define the program's metrics, write the BAA, run the proposers day, and drive selection. Understanding what a specific PM is trying to prove tells you more about your odds than any generic checklist.

The Heilmeier questions

DARPA's culture is shaped by the Heilmeier Catapult — a set of questions every program (and, by extension, every proposal) should answer plainly: What are you trying to do, in plain language? How is it done today and what are the limits? What's new in your approach and why will it succeed? Who cares? What are the risks and midterm and final exams for success? If your proposal answers these crisply, you're already ahead of most of the field.

How proposals are actually scored

Each BAA lists evaluation criteria and their relative importance — typically overall scientific and technical merit, relevance to the program's goals and metrics, the realism of the proposed cost and schedule, and the capability of the team. Proposals are evaluated on their own merit against these criteria, not ranked head-to-head, and selection is a best-value decision. Read those criteria as your grading rubric and write directly to them.

If your project needs simulation

Reviewers reward proposals that show, not tell — simulation results are one of the most efficient ways to show, and an Ansys eval through a partner helps produce them.

What consistently wins

Across offices, the same traits show up in winners: a sharp, testable hypothesis; genuine novelty (not an incremental improvement dressed up); honest treatment of risk with concrete mitigations; and evidence — data, prototypes, or simulation — that the hard part is achievable. Teams that hand-wave the scariest technical risk lose to teams that confront it with numbers.

Before you write

Engage early through a proposers day or DARPAConnect, and structure your response around the BAA's criteria — see our step-by-step on reading and responding to a DARPA BAA.
