SBIR grants vs. contracts: two very different games.

Updated July 28, 2026 · Free educational guide · verify details at the official sources linked below

SBIR agencies split roughly in half: NSF, DOE, USDA and most of NIH award grants — you propose your own R&D and draw funds flexibly. DoD, NASA, DHS, DOT and EPA award contracts — the agency publishes topics, you deliver milestones and invoice. Grants suit self-directed science; contracts suit teams that want a government customer from day one.

The split, agency by agency

Grants (you propose the idea)Contracts (they define the topic)
NSF · DOE · USDA · NIH (mostly)DoD · NASA · DHS · DOT · EPA · NIH (some topics)

How a grant feels

You bring the idea. If reviewers like the science and the commercial story, you get a Notice of Award and draw down funds as you spend. Scope changes are negotiable; reporting is periodic; the agency acts more like a patron than a customer. NSF is the purest version — tech-agnostic, equity-free, and explicitly friendly to first-time, pre-revenue founders. The catch: nobody at a grant agency is waiting to buy your product afterward.

How a contract feels

The agency publishes a topic describing a problem it wants solved. You propose against it, and if you win, you're a government contractor: defined deliverables, milestone schedules, invoicing (DoD pays through its WAWF system), and a named technical point of contact. It's more paperwork and less freedom — but that TPOC is a real customer inside the world's largest buyer, and DoD's Phase III sole-source authority means a successful prototype can turn into production revenue without another competition.

Which should a first-timer pick?

  • Deep-tech with broad commercial markets (materials, instruments, energy devices, software-adjacent hardware): start with NSF — the Project Pitch costs you two pages and no registrations.
  • Defense-relevant, dual-use hardware (UAVs, RF, space, autonomy): go where the customer is — DoD, starting with open topics at AFWERX or your service's cycle. See the DoD SBIR guide.
  • Energy and grid tech: DOE grants, with national-lab partnerships as a bonus. Compared in NSF vs NASA vs DOE.
  • Medical devices: NIH — grants culture, big budgets, regulatory-aware reviewers. See the NIH guide.
If your project needs simulation

Contract agencies write topics full of phrases like 'validated through modeling and simulation' — the deliverable often is the simulation study. If your target topic reads that way, having Ansys and an experienced application engineer lined up before award is the difference between month one and month four. Ask us what an eval could cover.

One warning that applies to both

You can apply to multiple agencies — even for related work — as long as you disclose overlaps and only accept money once for the same scope. Smart founders often run an NSF pitch and a DoD topic in parallel and take whichever lands first.
