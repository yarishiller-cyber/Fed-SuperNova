How to Register for Army SBIR and xTech: SAM.gov, SBIR.gov, and DSIP

Updated July 29, 2026 · Free educational guide · verify details at the official sources below

Founders lose winnable Army opportunities for a boring reason: their registrations were not finished in time. The paperwork is not hard, but parts of it are slow, and you cannot submit a proposal without it. Start these the day you decide to pursue Army funding.

Founders lose winnable Army opportunities for a boring reason: their registrations were not finished in time. The paperwork is not hard, but parts of it are slow, and you cannot submit a proposal without it. Start these the day you decide to pursue Army funding.

1. SAM.gov — get your Unique Entity ID first

Every federal awardee must be registered in SAM.gov (the System for Award Management), which issues your Unique Entity ID (UEI) and CAGE code. This is the long pole in the tent: allow 2–4+ weeks, because entity validation can stall on document mismatches. Use your exact legal business name and address as they appear on official records. Registration is free — never pay a third party for it.

2. SBIR.gov — the company registry

Register your firm in the SBIR.gov company registry to receive your SBC Control ID. Every SBIR/STTR applicant needs one, and it certifies your small-business eligibility (US-owned, for-profit, 500 or fewer employees). This step is quick once your basic company details are in hand.

3. DSIP — where Army SBIR proposals are submitted

Army SBIR/STTR proposals go through the DoD SBIR/STTR Innovation Portal (DSIP). Create your account, register your firm, add team members, and connect your SAM UEI and SBIR.gov control ID. Build your proposal in DSIP well before the deadline — the portal load spikes in the final hours of a solicitation, and late is late.

4. xTech — a lighter lift

xTech competitions are run as prize competitions and typically start with a short white paper submitted through the xTech site (xtech.army.mil), not DSIP. You generally do not need the full SBIR registration stack just to submit an xTech white paper — but if your win routes into a follow-on SBIR, you will need SAM, SBIR.gov, and DSIP anyway. So do them early regardless.

Do these in parallel, in this order of urgency

  1. SAM.gov — start immediately (slowest).
  2. SBIR.gov — do while SAM validates.
  3. DSIP — set up as soon as a solicitation you want is open.
  4. xTech — register per competition when it opens.

Keep everything active, not just created

Registration is not one-and-done. SAM.gov must be renewed annually, and an expired SAM entry will block an award even if you already won — verify it is active before every proposal. Keep your points of contact, banking (for payment), and NAICS codes current, and make sure the person who will sign has the right roles assigned in each system. A five-minute check a week before a deadline routinely saves a submission.

What you need in hand before you start

Have your exact legal business name and address, your EIN, incorporation documents, banking details for SAM, and a company email that is not tied to one departing employee. Decide who your authorized organizational representative is early — several steps require that specific person to act, and chasing a signature during the final hour of a solicitation is a preventable way to miss it.

Confirm current requirements at armysbir.army.mil and dodsbirsttr.mil before you begin — portal names and steps do change. The rule of thumb never does: register early, because the deadline will not move for your paperwork.
