Army xTech: cash prizes with no strings, and a door into Army SBIR

Updated July 29, 2026 · Free educational guide · verify details at the official sources below

Army xTech is a franchise of open- and themed-topic prize competitions that pay U.S. businesses cash for promising technology — non-dilutive, with no contract or IP strings attached. Winners also earn an exclusive invitation to submit an Army SBIR Phase I or Direct-to-Phase-II proposal.

The one-sentence version

Army xTech is how the U.S. Army finds technology it didn't know existed. It runs a family of prize competitions — open-topic ones like xTechSearch and themed ones like xTechPacific, xTech|Inversion, xTech|Kinetic Reach and xTech|Edge Strike: Ground — and pays cash to the best ideas.

Why the money is different

The prize is non-dilutive. You don't give up equity, you don't sign a contract to collect it, and you keep your intellectual property. That's rare in defense funding, and it's why xTech is a natural first step for stealth teams and pre-incorporation founders who aren't ready to take on a government contract yet.

A typical themed round puts around $1M on the table — for example, roughly a dozen finalists at $20K each and a handful of winners at $150K each. The numbers vary by competition, so always read the specific RFI.

How a round runs

The pattern is consistent: you submit a short concept white paper; a shortlist of finalists is invited to a virtual or live pitch in front of Army and Department of War subject-matter experts; and the strongest teams are named winners, sometimes after a live experimentation event where Soldiers actually touch the hardware.

The real prize is the lane

The cash is nice. The lane is better. xTech winners earn an exclusive invitation to submit an Army SBIR Phase I proposal — up to ~$250K — or, if the tech is already mature, a Direct-to-Phase-II proposal worth up to ~$2M. That's the on-ramp from a prize check to a real, funded R&D program with a transition partner. For how that path works step by step, see the xTech-to-SBIR path.

Who should enter

Cash-prize rounds are often open to any U.S. business, which is unusually broad. The SBIR follow-on requires you to be a U.S. small business. If that's you, xTech is one of the lowest-friction ways to get the Army's attention — and get paid for it.

Official sources: xTech competitions · Army SBIR program. Figures change; confirm on the official page before relying on them.
