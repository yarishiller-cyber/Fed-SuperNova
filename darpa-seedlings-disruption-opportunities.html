DARPA seedlings & Disruption Opportunities

Updated July 29, 2026 · Free educational guide · verify details at the official sources below

Disruption Opportunities (issued under DARPA's Disruptioneering program announcement) and seedlings are small, fast awards — often ~$250K to ~$2M across a feasibility phase and an optional proof-of-concept phase — designed to test one high-risk idea quickly. They're a natural first step for a small team before a full program.

Why seedlings exist

Not every good idea is ready for a $30M program. DARPA uses small, fast awards — commonly called seedlings, and now formalized through the Disruptioneering program announcement — to test whether a single bold concept is even feasible. They exist precisely so a small team can prove an idea without a multi-year commitment on either side.

How a Disruption Opportunity is structured

Individual Disruption Opportunities are released under the standing Disruptioneering announcement. Each typically has two phases: a Phase 1 feasibility study and an optional Phase 2 proof of concept, awarded only if Phase 1 delivers. Combined funding is usually in the range of a few hundred thousand to a couple of million dollars, over a performance period on the order of a year or two. Amounts and periods vary by DO, so read each one.

Why this suits startups

The abstract is short, the timeline is fast, and the bar is feasibility rather than a finished capability. For a stealth or pre-incorporation team, that's ideal: you can win credibility and non-dilutive money by answering the single scariest technical question about your idea.

If your project needs simulation

Feasibility is exactly where simulation earns its keep — an Ansys evaluation through a channel partner can buy down technical risk cheaper than building hardware.

What a feasibility case looks like

DARPA doesn't expect a working system from a seedling — it expects convincing evidence that one could exist. That evidence is often analytical and computational: a model showing the thermal loads are survivable, a simulation showing the antenna can hit the bandwidth, a stress analysis showing the novel structure won't fail. Producing that evidence quickly, before you've spent your budget on hardware, is the whole point.

How to move

Watch the Disruptioneering announcement and the events calendar for open DOs, submit an abstract by the DO's deadline, and be ready to move fast if invited to a full proposal. If your idea is bigger than a feasibility test, compare this path with a full program in our guide to responding to a DARPA BAA.

Official sources: DARPA — Disruptioneering · DARPA-PA-25-07 program announcement. Figures change; confirm on the official page before relying on them.
