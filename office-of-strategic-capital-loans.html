The DoD Office of Strategic Capital: loans, not grants

Updated July 29, 2026 · Free educational guide · verify details at the official sources below

The DoD Office of Strategic Capital (OSC) provides direct loans and loan guarantees — not grants — of $10M to $150M to finance manufacturing equipment and facilities for critical technologies with defense and commercial uses. FY2026 loan capacity is about $4.4B across ~31 covered technology categories, and applications open through Notices of Funding Availability (NOFAs). Its first loan went to MP Materials in 2025.

Debt, not a check you keep

The Office of Strategic Capital is the newest and most misunderstood tool in defense scale-up. It's inside OUSD(R&E), and it does something the rest of the ecosystem mostly doesn't: it lends. Direct loans and loan guarantees, roughly $10M to $150M each, to finance the manufacturing equipment and facilities behind critical technologies. It is non-dilutive in the sense that you don't give up equity — but it's debt, and you pay it back.

OSC was set up because a lot of hard-tech manufacturing needs patient, large capital that private lenders won't touch on their own timelines. Congress authorized about $4.4B of FY2026 loan capacity, and the FY2027 budget request pushes the loan program well above $20B — a sign of how fast this is scaling.

The ~31 covered categories

You can't borrow for just anything. OSC works within roughly 31 covered technology categories — advanced manufacturing, microelectronics, edge computing, quantum, battery storage, advanced materials, mesh networks, and more. A hard requirement: your investment must have both a defense and a commercial application. Pure-defense or pure-commercial plays don't fit.

How you actually get in

Access is through Notices of Funding Availability (NOFAs), published in the Federal Register. The first, an equipment-finance NOFA in late 2024, authorized up to ~$984M and drew more than 200 applications totaling ~$8.9B across 38 states — so demand dwarfs supply. The process runs like project finance: a pre-application consultation, an Application Part 1, then deep, slow, finance-grade diligence. OSC executed its first loan with MP Materials in 2025 to shore up rare-earth supply.

What underwriters actually scrutinize

Because this is lending, the diligence looks like a bank's, not a grant panel's. Expect hard questions about your ability to repay: realistic revenue, offtake or purchase commitments, the collateral value of the equipment being financed, and management's track record. The dual-use test matters here too — a credible commercial market alongside the defense demand is what convinces an underwriter the loan survives even if a single program slips. Vague projections and a single-customer story are what sink applications.

The equipment focus is a feature, not a limitation. Loans tied to specific, valuable manufacturing assets are easier to underwrite because the assets themselves back the debt. If you can point to exactly which machines, lines, or facility the money buys — and what they're worth — you're speaking the underwriter's language.

Plan for the timeline

The single biggest founder mistake is treating OSC like a grant you'll have in 90 days. It isn't. Underwriting a $50M-plus loan takes real time and real financials. Line it up early, ideally alongside an APFIT buy that proves demand, and use the wait to de-risk your production ramp so the numbers you underwrite against actually hold.

Official sources: OSC (cto.mil/osc) · Congressional Research Service: OSC overview · OSC first loan — MP Materials. Figures change; confirm on the official page before relying on them.
