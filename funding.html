Grant

NIH SBIR/STTR

The single largest source of non-dilutive money for U.S. health small businesses — roughly $1.3B a year across the NIH institutes, and medical devices are squarely in scope.

  • Phase I ~$314K–$323K; Phase II ~$2.15M (higher on waiver topics) · Non-dilutive
  • For-profit U.S. small business (<500 employees), majority U.S.-owned; PI primarily employed by the company for SBIR.
  • Standard due dates ~Jan 5, Apr 5, Sep 5

How to apply: Pick the right institute (NHLBI, NIBIB, NCI, NIA...), respond to the omnibus or a targeted NOFO, register in SAM.gov/eRA Commons, and submit through Grants.gov. Contact the program officer before you write.

Grant

NIH SBIR Phase IIB / Strategic Breakthrough

A follow-on bridge for technologies that finished a Phase II and need to cross the valley to commercialization — clinical validation, manufacturing scale-up, regulatory work.

  • Up to ~$30M total support on the newest Strategic Breakthrough track (varies by institute) · Non-dilutive
  • Prior NIH SBIR/STTR Phase II awardee with a clear commercialization path and, often, matching third-party investment.
  • annual / institute-specific

How to apply: Work with your institute's SBIR program staff; many Phase IIB tracks require a committed investor or strategic partner match.

Contract

ARPA-H SBIR/STTR

The health 'DARPA' runs an SBIR program with the most generous caps anywhere — built for high-risk, high-reward device ideas that mainstream reviewers might call too ambitious.

  • Up to ~$600K Phase I and ~$3.5M Phase II · Non-dilutive
  • U.S. small business; milestone-driven contracts tied to ARPA-H's aggressive go/no-go structure.
  • rolling per solicitation (Solution Summaries by posted dates)

How to apply: Watch the SBIR page and SAM.gov, submit a Solution Summary against an open topic, then a full proposal if invited.

OTA

ARPA-H Open BAA (OTs & cooperative agreements)

ARPA-H's core funding is not classic grants at all — it's Other Transactions and cooperative agreements awarded against mission-focused program solicitations and the open BAA.

  • Program-dependent; often multi-million, milestone-gated · Non-dilutive
  • Broad — companies, universities, nonprofits, teams; ARPANET-H hubs (incl. the Investor Catalyst Hub run by VentureWell) help performers connect.
  • rolling (open BAA) + targeted program solicitations

How to apply: Respond to a specific ARPA-H program solicitation or the open BAA on SAM.gov; abstracts first, then full proposals.

Contract

BARDA DRIVe EZ-BAA

A fast, lightweight on-ramp into BARDA for early-stage health-security tech — including devices and diagnostics — with a deliberately short application.

  • Up to ~$750K non-dilutive · Non-dilutive
  • U.S. entities addressing a DRIVe area of interest (early detection, sepsis, resilience, etc.); pilot-scale scope.
  • rolling submissions against open EZ-BAA areas of interest

How to apply: Read the current EZ-BAA, match an area of interest, and submit the short proposal package on the DRIVe portal.

Contract

BARDA DRIVe BAA (Special Instructions)

For programs that outgrow the EZ-BAA — larger, multi-phase medical-countermeasure and device development with substantial funding and BARDA's advanced-development support.

  • Scales well beyond EZ-BAA, up to ~$20M+ per program · Non-dilutive
  • Entities with a defensible development plan aligned to BARDA's preparedness mission.
  • rolling; white paper then full proposal

How to apply: Submit an abstract/white paper under the BAA Special Instructions; invited teams submit full proposals.

OTA

MTEC (Medical Technology Enterprise Consortium)

A nonprofit consortium that channels Army USAMRDC and Defense Health Agency dollars into medical technology through fast Other Transaction agreements — a direct defense-to-medical-device bridge.

  • $50K–$25M per award (RPP-dependent; recent device RPPs ~$2M–$3.5M) · Non-dilutive
  • MTEC members (small/large business, academia, nonprofits); membership is inexpensive and open.
  • Request for Project Proposals (RPPs) issued through the year

How to apply: Join the consortium, monitor RPPs on the member portal, and respond with a white paper to a matching RPP.

Grant

NIH REACH Hubs (proof-of-concept)

Regional academic entrepreneurship hubs that fund the earliest product-definition work — feasibility, prototypes, proof-of-concept — plus regulatory, reimbursement, and business coaching.

  • Project awards vary; hubs funded at ~$1M/yr each for four years · Non-dilutive
  • Innovators affiliated with a REACH-hub institution; academic device projects with commercial potential.
  • hub-run internal cycles

How to apply: Find your nearest hub through NIH SEED and pitch a technology-development project into its internal competition.

Official source ↗

Grant

I-Corps at NIH

An intensive customer-discovery boot camp — 100+ interviews — that de-risks the market before you burn SBIR money, and strengthens the commercialization section of your next application.

  • Modest training grant (~$50K) + curriculum · Non-dilutive
  • Active or recent NIH SBIR/STTR Phase I awardees (and REACH-affiliated teams via the network).
  • cohorts, typically spring and fall

How to apply: Ask your program officer about the current cohort; apply as a three-person team (PI, entrepreneurial lead, mentor).

Official source ↗

Grant

NSF SBIR/STTR (America's Seed Fund)

NSF funds deep-tech and hardware startups, medical devices included, when the innovation is technically risky and the science is strong — a non-HHS alternative worth stacking.

  • Phase I up to ~$305K; Phase II up to ~$1M (+ supplements) · Non-dilutive
  • U.S. for-profit small business with a technically innovative, scalable product (no clinical trials via NSF).
  • Project Pitch rolling; full proposal by invitation

How to apply: Submit a 3-page Project Pitch first; if invited, submit the full Phase I proposal.

The lifecycle

How funding flows — and where simulation fits

01

Concept / Pre-seed (idea, stealth, pre-incorp)

~$0–$50K (I-Corps, REACH, accelerators) · 3–9 months

Prove there's a real clinical need and a buyer, and that the physics could plausibly work.

Cheap, fast feasibility studies — Ansys Discovery for first-pass CFD/FEA on a concept — replace expensive early prototypes and sharpen the technical claims in your first proposal.

02

Phase I — feasibility

~$314K–$600K (NIH/ARPA-H SBIR) · 6–12 months

Demonstrate technical feasibility of the core device concept against defined metrics.

Stand up your verification & validation plan now: build the first credibility-graded models (ASME V&V 40 vocabulary) so Phase II reviewers see a device that's already being de-risked in silico.

03

Phase II — development & prototype

~$2M–$3.5M (NIH/ARPA-H SBIR, MTEC) · 2 years

Build a working prototype and generate preclinical performance and safety data.

Assemble the full computational-credibility package the FDA guidance expects — verification, validation against bench data, and applicability — for stress, fatigue, hemodynamics, or RF-safety claims.

04

Bridge / scale (Phase IIB, BARDA BAA, MTEC OTA)

~$3M–$25M · 2–4 years

Clinical validation, manufacturing scale-up, and regulatory groundwork.

Use in-silico trials with virtual-patient cohorts to supplement clinical enrollment, and consider qualifying a model through the FDA MDDT program so it counts across submissions.

05

Submission & commercialization

Revenue / follow-on / strategic · ongoing

Clear the FDA (510(k), De Novo, or PMA) and get to market.

Deliver the computational modeling & simulation credibility evidence inside the submission itself — the November 2023 CDRH guidance tells reviewers exactly how to weigh it.
