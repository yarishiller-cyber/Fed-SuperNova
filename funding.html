DARPA Program BAAs

Each active DARPA program publishes a Broad Agency Announcement soliciting proposals against a specific hard technical goal. This is the primary path to a full performer award.

  • ~$5M–$50M per team · Non-dilutive
  • Open to all responsible sources — startups, primes, universities, nonprofits. No small-business set-aside.
  • rolling (per program)

How to apply: Read the BAA on SAM.gov, submit an abstract first (strongly encouraged), and only write the full proposal if the PM signals interest.

Office-Wide BAAs

Each of DARPA's technical offices maintains a standing office-wide BAA for ideas that fit the office mission but aren't tied to one named program. A way in when no program BAA fits.

  • ~$500K–$10M · Non-dilutive
  • Open to all performer types across the sponsoring office's mission space.
  • rolling (refreshed annually)

How to apply: Find the relevant office (DSO, MXO, STO, TTO, BTO, IPTO), submit an abstract to the listed BAA, and engage the office's technical staff.

Disruption Opportunities (Disruptioneering, DARPA-PA-25-07)

Fast, small feasibility awards for a single bold idea. Phase 1 is a feasibility study; Phase 2 is a proof of concept if the first phase delivers.

  • ~$250K–$2M (combined phases) · Non-dilutive
  • Open to all; well suited to small teams and university groups that can move fast.
  • rolling (individual DOs open and close)

How to apply: Watch the Disruptioneering Program Announcement for open DOs, submit an abstract by the DO's deadline, then a full proposal if invited.

Official source ↗

Embedded Entrepreneurship Initiative (EEI)

Catalytic, non-dilutive funding for an existing DARPA performer to hire an embedded entrepreneur and build a real commercialization and go-to-market strategy for defense and commercial markets.

  • ~$250K–$340K · Non-dilutive
  • Current DARPA-funded research teams with commercialization potential.
  • by cohort / invitation

How to apply: Discuss with your program manager and the Commercial Strategy Office; selection is based on your commercialization strategy and team.

Official source ↗

SBIR (Small Business Innovation Research)

Federal set-aside program for small businesses. DARPA publishes topics; awards ladder from Phase I feasibility to Phase II development, with Phase III transition to non-SBIR funding.

  • ~$150K (Ph I) to ~$2M+ (Ph II) · Non-dilutive
  • U.S. small businesses (majority U.S.-owned, <500 employees).
  • multiple topic releases per year

How to apply: Find DARPA topics on DSIP, use the pre-release window to ask the topic author questions, then submit through dodsbirsttr.mil.

Official source ↗

Contract

STTR (Small Business Technology Transfer)

Like SBIR, but requires a formal partnership between a small business and a research institution (university or federal lab), with minimum work shares for each.

  • ~$150K (Ph I) to ~$2M+ (Ph II) · Non-dilutive
  • U.S. small business + a qualifying U.S. research institution partner.
  • multiple topic releases per year

How to apply: Line up your research-institution partner early, then submit against a DARPA STTR topic via DSIP.

Official source ↗

AI Exploration (AIE)

A streamlined program-announcement mechanism for high-risk, high-payoff AI concepts, structured for very fast award and a short performance period.

  • ~$1M · Non-dilutive
  • Open to all; sized for small, fast-moving teams.
  • rolling (individual AIE opportunities)

How to apply: Respond to a specific AIE opportunity with an abstract on the compressed timeline the announcement sets.

Official source ↗

OTA

Other Transaction Agreements (OTs)

Flexible prototype and production agreements outside the standard FAR contract, used to bring in nontraditional performers and startups with less overhead.

  • varies by prototype · Non-dilutive
  • Especially favorable to nontraditional defense contractors and startups.
  • per program

How to apply: OTs are usually offered within a program; signal your nontraditional status and prototype focus during BAA engagement.

Official source ↗

Young Faculty Award (YFA)

Funds rising early-career university researchers to explore DARPA-relevant ideas and build a relationship with the agency.

  • ~$500K over ~2 years · Non-dilutive
  • Untenured junior faculty and equivalent early-career researchers at U.S. institutions.
  • annual

How to apply: Submit an executive summary against the annual YFA solicitation, then a full proposal if encouraged.

Official source ↗

Prize

DARPA Prize Challenges

Open competitions (robotics, cyber, spectrum, subterranean autonomy and more) that pay for demonstrated capability rather than a proposal — a way to earn visibility and cash without a traditional award.

  • prize pools up to ~$millions · Non-dilutive
  • Varies by challenge; many are open to newcomers and self-funded teams.
  • per challenge

How to apply: Register for an active challenge and compete against the published rules and milestones.

The lifecycle

How funding flows — and where simulation fits

01

Abstract & PM engagement

$0 · weeks

Proves your idea fits a program's goal and is worth a full proposal — the abstract is where most teams are filtered out.

A single clean simulation figure (a stress plot, a CFD field, an antenna pattern) in the abstract signals the physics is real, not hand-waved.

02

Seedling / Disruption Opportunity

~$250K–$2M · ~6–24 months

Feasibility: prove the core physics is plausible and de-risk the one thing everyone doubts.

Simulation is often the cheapest way to buy down feasibility risk before you build any hardware — and to show a Phase 2 case.

03

Full program performer

~$5M–$50M · ~2–4 years

Build and demonstrate the capability against hard, PM-defined milestones and quantitative metrics.

Digital-twin and high-fidelity models let you hit metrics with fewer physical test articles and explain misses to your PM.

04

Commercialization (EEI)

~$250K–$340K · ~1–2 years

Turn a working prototype into a business with a real go-to-market plan for defense and commercial customers.

Validated models become a durable engineering asset investors and dual-use customers can actually evaluate.

05

Transition & production

varies (OT / follow-on) · ongoing

Move the technology to a service, program of record, or commercial product via OTs and follow-on contracts.

Model-based qualification shortens the certification and production-scale-up path once demand is real.
