Every DoD OTA Consortia — How to Join & Win door, in one place.

Real programs, real numbers, real official links — verified July 29, 2026. Amounts and deadlines change constantly, so confirm on each official source before you rely on it. Not sure which fits what you're building? Ask us.

OTA

S2MARTS (NSTXL)

Strategic & Spectrum Missions Advanced Resilient Trusted Systems — the premier rapid-OT vehicle for trusted microelectronics, RF/spectrum and strategic mission systems, sponsored out of NSWC Crane. Solicitations are called Requests for Solutions (RFS).

  • ~$250K–$100M+ per prototype · Non-dilutive
  • NSTXL members (industry, non-traditional, academia); a free S2MARTS Research membership tier exists for research-only participation.
  • rolling RFS releases

How to apply: Join NSTXL online (CAGE + UEI, approved in days), then respond to an open RFS with a short solution brief; strong ones are invited to full proposal.

SpEC — Space Enterprise Consortium (NSTXL)

The Space Systems Command vehicle for space prototypes — satellites, ground systems, launch, resilient comms. 600+ members, ~70% non-traditional; solicitations here are Requests for Prototype Proposals (RPPs).

  • ~$500K–$100M+ per prototype · Non-dilutive
  • NSTXL members with expertise in a SpEC mission area; startups and small businesses explicitly courted.
  • rolling RPP releases

How to apply: One NSTXL membership covers SpEC; respond to an RPP with a white paper, then an invited full proposal.

MTEC — Medical Technology Enterprise Consortium

Military-medicine prototypes under an OTA with the Army Medical Research & Development Command (USAMRDC): trauma care, wearables, diagnostics, regenerative medicine, behavioral health.

  • ~$50K–$25M (typical initial ~$2–3.5M) · Non-dilutive
  • MTEC members; ~95 institutions and several hundred industry partners. Dues ~$1,000 small biz / academia / non-profit, ~$5,000 large business.
  • annual multi-topic RFP + targeted RPPs

How to apply: Join MTEC, respond to a solicitation; note a 2% assessment fee is owed to MTEC on funded awards, due ~90 days after obligation.

SOSSEC — System of Systems Consortium

A veteran consortium manager running eight-plus OTAs across C4ISR, GEOINT, sensors/comms/electronics and space startups. 700+ members.

  • varies by OTA (~$100K–$50M) · Non-dilutive
  • One ~$500/yr SOSSEC membership gives access to every SOSSEC-managed OTA at once.
  • rolling across member OTAs

How to apply: Register on the SOSSEC portal, then respond to project announcements under whichever member OTA fits your tech.

DIBC — Defense Industrial Base Consortium (ATI)

Feeds DPA Title III (DPA Investments) and IBAS/ICAM industrial-base funding: critical minerals, castings/forgings, microelectronics capacity, energetics, batteries. Accepts unsolicited white papers plus targeted RPPs.

  • ~$500K–$100M+ (industrial-base scale) · Non-dilutive
  • Open to US organizations plus UK, Australia and Canada partners. Membership is free today (dues capped at ~$250 if ever introduced; HBCUs/MSIs exempt).
  • open announcement + periodic RPPs

How to apply: Join free, then submit a white paper to the open announcement or a specific RPP.

IWRP 3 — Information Warfare Research Project (ATI)

NIWC Atlantic's information-warfare OTA: cyber, C4ISR, cloud, AI/ML, assured comms and EW prototypes for Navy and Marine Corps. 580+ partners; 2024–2034 ordering period.

  • ~$250K–$50M per prototype · Non-dilutive
  • Open membership for US industry, academia and non-profits.
  • rolling RPP releases

How to apply: Join IWRP, watch for RPPs in your focus area, submit a white paper then invited proposal.

TReX II — Training & Readiness Accelerator (ATI)

The Army CPE ST3 vehicle for modeling, simulation and training prototypes — synthetic environments, LVC, XR, wargaming and medical training.

  • ~$250K–$25M per prototype · Non-dilutive
  • US companies or US-based affiliates only; a DD2345 (militarily critical technical data) agreement is required. Dues ~$500/yr.

How to apply: Join TReX II, obtain your DD2345, then respond to an RFS.

NAC — National Armaments Consortium (ATI / DOTC)

The Army/Navy DOTC OTA for armaments and munitions: energetics, weapons, fire control, guidance. One of the largest OTAs by ceiling; 900+ members across the network.

  • ~$1M–$100M+ per prototype · Non-dilutive
  • US-based NAC members with a DD2345; strong ITAR/export-control posture expected.
  • rolling white-paper requests

The lifecycle

How funding flows — and where simulation fits

01

1. Join the consortium

$0–$500/yr dues · days to ~2 weeks

Establishes eligibility. Get a CAGE code and UEI, sign the membership agreement (and a DD2345 for armaments/training OTAs). One fee often covers a whole family of OTAs.

Nothing physics-heavy yet — but this is when you decide which prototype you'll pitch, and that decision determines the simulation you'll need to back it up.

02

2. White paper / RFS response

unfunded (your time) · 2–6 weeks per response

A short concept paper against an RPP or RFS: the problem, your approach, why it's feasible, rough cost and schedule. This is the real gate — most teams are filtered here.

A single credible simulation figure — a thermal map, a stress plot, an antenna pattern — separates a hand-wave white paper from one that reads as executable.

03

3. Invited full proposal

unfunded · 3–8 weeks

Only strong white papers are invited. Now you detail the technical plan, milestones, deliverables, team and cost. Government evaluates and negotiates.

Reviewers want evidence you can hit milestones. Simulation-backed feasibility, margin and risk-reduction analysis is exactly the evidence that de-risks your plan on paper.

04

4. Prototype OT award

~$250K–$25M+ · 6 months–3 years

A non-dilutive prototype Other Transaction. You build and demonstrate. No FAR/DFARS overhead cascade; flexible IP terms; milestone payments.

This is where simulation earns its keep — trade studies, design iterations and virtual test reduce how many expensive physical builds you burn to hit the demo.

05

5. Follow-on production OT

$M–$B (program-dependent) · multi-year

A successful prototype can convert to a production OT non-competitively — no re-competition. This is the whole reason the model exists: a real path off the SBIR treadmill into scaled procurement.

At production scale, simulation moves to reliability, manufacturability and sustainment — fatigue, tolerance, thermal margin across the fielded fleet.
