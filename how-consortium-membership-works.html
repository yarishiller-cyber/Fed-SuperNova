How consortium membership actually works

Updated July 29, 2026 · Free educational guide · verify details at the official sources below

To bid on most defense OTAs you must first be a consortium member. That means a CAGE code and UEI (both free via SAM.gov), a signed membership agreement, low annual dues (often $250–$1,000, sometimes free), and — for armaments or training OTAs — a DD2345. Approval typically takes days, not months.

Step 1: Get your federal identifiers

Before any consortium can approve you, register on SAM.gov to get a UEI (Unique Entity ID) and a CAGE code. Both are free. This is also where idea-stage founders hit reality: you generally need a legal entity to register, so a stealth team should plan to incorporate before award, even if you can research everything first.

Step 2: Pick and join the consortium

Choose by technology fit (see the consortium map). Joining is usually an online application plus your CAGE/UEI and a signed Consortium Membership Agreement. NSTXL and others often approve in days.

Step 3: Pay the dues

They're deliberately low. DIBC is free. NSTXL tiers from ~$250. SOSSEC, TReX and MCDC are around $500/yr. MTEC is ~$1,000 small / ~$5,000 large, and charges a 2% assessment on funded awards. One fee often covers a whole family of OTAs.

If your project needs simulation

Membership makes you eligible; a credible prototype makes you fundable. An Ansys eval license through a partner helps you show the physics is real from your very first white paper.

Step 4: Handle export-control paperwork

Armaments (NAC) and training (TReX) OTAs require a DD2345 — a Militarily Critical Technical Data Agreement — and these are usually US-only. If you touch ITAR-controlled tech, get your compliance house in order early; it's a common reason otherwise-good teams stall.

Step 5: Get inside the deal flow

Membership unlocks the member portal, industry days and technical exchange meetings where RPPs and RFS are previewed. Show up — the teams that win usually heard the requirement taking shape before it was published. Then read how to write a winning white paper.

Official sources: NSTXL — can my firm join? · TReX II — how to join · SAM.gov. Figures change; confirm on the official page before relying on them.
