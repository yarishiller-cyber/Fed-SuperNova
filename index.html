Funding, mapped

A few of the programs

Grant

NIH SBIR/STTR

The single largest source of non-dilutive money for U.S. health small businesses — roughly $1.3B a year across the NIH institutes, and medical devices are squarely in scope.

Phase I ~$314K–$323K; Phase II ~$2.15M (higher on waiver topics) · Non-dilutive

Grant

NIH SBIR Phase IIB / Strategic Breakthrough

A follow-on bridge for technologies that finished a Phase II and need to cross the valley to commercialization — clinical validation, manufacturing scale-up, regulatory work.

Up to ~$30M total support on the newest Strategic Breakthrough track (varies by institute) · Non-dilutive

Contract

ARPA-H SBIR/STTR

The health 'DARPA' runs an SBIR program with the most generous caps anywhere — built for high-risk, high-reward device ideas that mainstream reviewers might call too ambitious.

Up to ~$600K Phase I and ~$3.5M Phase II · Non-dilutive

What you'll simulate

Ansys applications for this ecosystem

01 · STRUCTURAL

Stent & implant deployment and fatigue life

Model balloon expansion, recoil, and the millions of cardiac or gait cycles an implant must survive. Predict peak stress, residual strain, and fracture risk before a single sample hits the durability rig. This is the classic FEA claim the FDA expects to see backed by a credibility case.

MechanicalLS-DYNA
02 · HEMODYNAMICS

Blood pump & LVAD flow and hemolysis

Simulate the pressure-flow curve of an impeller and estimate shear-driven hemolysis and thrombosis risk in blood-contacting hardware. FDA has published benchmark CFD problems for exactly this class of device, so validated flow models carry real regulatory weight.

FluentMechanical
03 · STRUCTURAL-FLUID

Heart valve & TAVI device–anatomy interaction

Couple the fluid and the structure (FSI) to see how a transcatheter valve seats against patient anatomy, how leaflets flex, and where paravalvular leak or calcium interaction shows up. Patient-specific runs feed straight into in-silico trial cohorts.
