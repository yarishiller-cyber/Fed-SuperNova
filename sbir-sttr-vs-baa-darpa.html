DARPA SBIR/STTR vs. a program BAA

Updated July 29, 2026 · Free educational guide · verify details at the official sources below

SBIR/STTR are small-business set-asides with a phased structure (feasibility, then development) submitted through DSIP; program BAAs are open to all performers and fund larger, program-defined work. SBIR/STTR is often the cleanest on-ramp for a small team; a BAA suits teams ready to take on a full program metric.

Two doors into DARPA

If you're a small company, you have two main ways in: the SBIR/STTR programs, and open program (or office-wide) BAAs. They overlap but reward different situations.

SBIR and STTR: the small-business on-ramp

SBIR is a federal set-aside for U.S. small businesses; STTR is the same idea but requires a formal partnership with a research institution and sets minimum work shares for each partner. Both are phased: Phase I proves feasibility (smaller, shorter), Phase II funds development, and Phase III transitions the technology using non-SBIR money. DARPA publishes topics you respond to, and all proposals go through the Defense SBIR/STTR Innovation Portal (DSIP). During the pre-release window you can talk directly to the topic author — use it.

Program BAAs: bigger, broader, open to all

A program BAA is not a set-aside. Startups, universities, and primes compete on the same footing to perform work defined by a DARPA program, and awards can be far larger than a typical SBIR. You're proposing against the program's specific metrics rather than a narrow topic, which means more scope — and more risk if your team is small.

If your project needs simulation

Both paths reward preliminary technical evidence — an Ansys evaluation through a channel partner helps a small team show feasibility in either.

How to choose

If you're small, early, and want a structured phased path with a lower entry bar, SBIR/STTR is usually the cleaner start — and a Phase I win is real credibility. If your idea maps directly onto an active program's goal and you can staff a full effort, a program BAA (or a fast Disruption Opportunity) may be the better fit. Many teams do both over time: land an SBIR, build evidence, and parlay it into a program role.
