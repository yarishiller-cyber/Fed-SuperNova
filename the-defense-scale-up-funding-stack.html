The defense scale-up funding stack

Updated July 29, 2026 · Free educational guide · verify details at the official sources below

Defense scale-up funding is a stack, not a single program: SBIR/STTR proves the tech (non-dilutive R&D), SSBCI provides early state capital, APFIT buys production units (non-dilutive procurement), the Office of Strategic Capital lends $10M–$150M to build the factory (debt), and DPA Title III/IBAS invest to close industrial-base gaps. Sequencing them beats relying on any one.

Stop thinking about one program

New defense founders tend to hunt for 'the' funding program. There isn't one. Scale-up is a stack of tools, each solving a different stage, and the companies that survive learn to sequence them. Here's the map.

The layers

Prove it — SBIR/STTR. Non-dilutive R&D contracts, Phase I around $75K–$250K and Phase II often $0.75M–$2M+, that fund the prototype and demonstration everything else is built on. A strong Phase II is what makes you nominatable later. dodsbirsttr.mil

Early capital — SSBCI. State-run equity co-invest and loans, funded by a ~$10B Treasury program. The quiet first check most founders miss.

Buy it — APFIT. Non-dilutive procurement of production units, averaging >$30M, that crosses the valley of death. Remember you can't apply — you get nominated.

Build it — OSC loans. $10M–$150M in debt via NOFAs to finance the factory and equipment. Fix the base — DPA Title III / IBAS. Investment to close documented industrial-base gaps, often via the DIB Consortium.

Sequencing example

A typical path: SBIR Phase I/II proves the capability and wins an end user; SSBCI or private capital covers early burn; a program office nominates you for an APFIT buy; you finance the production line with an OSC loan; and if you're closing a national shortfall, DPA Title III adds capacity investment. Each layer de-risks the next.

Dilutive, non-dilutive, or debt — mix on purpose

A smart stack also balances what each dollar costs you. SBIR, APFIT, and DPA Title III are non-dilutive — you keep your equity and your cap table. OSC is debt — you keep equity but take on repayment. SSBCI and private venture are typically dilutive. The founders who scale cleanly use the non-dilutive layers to carry as much of the load as possible, take debt only against assets and revenue they can service, and save equity for the moments when speed genuinely beats ownership. Reaching for a priced round to cover something an APFIT buy or an SSBCI check could have funded is how you give away a company you didn't need to.

None of these layers is guaranteed, and none of them arrives on your schedule. Treat the stack as a portfolio: pursue several in parallel, expect some to fall through, and keep enough runway that no single 'no' is fatal.

The thread that runs through all of it

Every one of these dollars assumes your hardware performs at volume. That's why de-risking the production ramp with simulation isn't a side quest — it's the through-line that makes the whole stack fundable.

Official sources: APFIT program office · DoD Office of Strategic Capital · U.S. Treasury: SSBCI · OUSD(A&S) Industrial Base Policy. Figures change; confirm on the official page before relying on them.
