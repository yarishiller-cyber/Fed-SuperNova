"Free Ansys software" — the honest map of what exists and what it's for.

Updated July 29, 2026 · Free educational guide · verify details at the official sources linked below

There is no free commercial version of Ansys. What exists: free Ansys Student products (education only, size-capped, renewable 12-month license), official 30-day trials of several products, short-term full-capability evaluation licenses granted through Ansys and its channel partners to qualified prospects, the deeply discounted (not free) Ansys Startup Program for eligible early-stage companies, and a large library of genuinely free learning content.

Start with the sentence most articles bury

There is no free commercial version of Ansys. If you search "free Ansys software," most of what ranks is download tutorials for the student build, often with stale numbers, and none of it answers the question a founder is actually asking: how do I legitimately run real Ansys on my company's problem without paying list price on day one? That question has good answers. Here's the whole map, honestly labeled.

1 · Ansys Student — free, real, and fenced

The free student downloads are genuinely capable learning builds: the main Ansys Student bundle (Mechanical, Fluent, CFX, Discovery, SpaceClaim and more), Electronics Desktop Student (HFSS, Maxwell, Q3D, Icepak), LS-DYNA Student, and SCADE Student. Two fences to understand:

  • Size caps. Roughly 128K nodes/elements for structural work and ~512K cells for fluids in recent releases, with tighter caps in Electronics Desktop (64K elements for 3D volume solvers) — enough for coursework and self-training, cramped for real products. Check the live limits table on the official page; the numbers move between releases and most third-party articles quote old ones.
  • Education only. The license is for self-learning, student instruction, student projects and student demonstrations. Ansys's academic terms exclude commercial activity outright — no product design, no consulting, no proprietary work. A freelancer billing hours on Ansys Student is violating the license, full stop. The longer story is in our academic-licensing guide.

The license is embedded in each release and runs about 12 months — you renew by downloading the newest student release. Free support comes from the Ansys Learning Forum community.

2 · Official trials — self-serve, about 30 days

Several products have public free trials on ansys.com: Fluent (30 days, full-featured, including the GPU solver), Discovery (one 30-day trial per user), plus SpaceClaim, Sherlock, SCADE, and others. Trials are great first tastes — real software, no negotiation. Their limits are practical rather than legal: 30 days is enough to learn the interface and run a known example; it's rarely enough to prove out an unfamiliar multiphysics workflow on your own design while learning the tool. That's the job of the next entry.

3 · Evaluation licenses — the founder's actual answer

The path the download-tutorial articles never mention: Ansys and its authorized channel partners grant short-term evaluation licenses to qualified commercial prospects — typically at no cost, typically full-capability, scoped in a short conversation about what you need to prove. Arranged through a partner, an eval usually comes with an application engineer supporting it and free self-paced courses lined up for your tools. It's the difference between "I tried the trial" and "we validated our workflow on our own geometry before spending anything." Evals are granted at Ansys's and its partners' discretion, subject to eligibility and export screening — the full picture, including how to ask, is on our Ansys evals page.

If your project needs simulation

If you're a founder rather than a student, the entry on this map built for you is the evaluation license: the real, uncapped software on your real problem, typically at no cost to qualified prospects, with an application engineer supporting the eval and free self-paced courses alongside. It's granted at Ansys's and its partners' discretion — but asking costs nothing and the reply is fast.

4 · The Ansys Startup Program — discounted, not free, and worth it

For eligible early-stage companies — privately held, under 10 years old, under $5M in annual revenue, and not an engineering-consulting/simulation-services firm — the Ansys Startup Program sells bundled, full-capability commercial licenses at deep discounts, with Ansys Learning Hub training access and support included. Bundles cover structures & fluids, electromagnetics, optics & photonics, embedded software, and digital missions; over 1,850 startups across 57 countries have used the program. Pricing isn't public and varies by situation — which is exactly the kind of thing a five-minute conversation settles. This is the sanctioned long-term home for a company that outgrows evals but can't yet pay standard commercial pricing; see also how commercial licensing works.

5 · Free learning — no license needed at all

While you sort out software access, the learning layer is genuinely free: Ansys Innovation Courses (structured physics + simulation courses with videos and quizzes), the academic learning resources hub, and the official Ansys Learning channel on YouTube. A founder can be genuinely dangerous in Fluent or HFSS before ever holding a license.

The decision table

  • Student learning a tool? Ansys Student. Free, legal, size-capped, education only.
  • Curious professional? Official 30-day trial of Fluent or Discovery.
  • Founder with a real problem to prove? Ask about an evaluation license — real software, real workflow, engineer attached. Start here.
  • Eligible startup ready for its own licenses? Startup Program pricing, then standard commercial licensing as you scale — and if you're chasing SBIR, award dollars can generally carry project software costs.

Official sources for this guide: Ansys for Students (official, with live limits table) · Ansys Fluent free trial · Ansys Startup Program · Ansys Innovation Courses (free) · Ansys academic usage terms. Figures change; always confirm on the official page before relying on them.
