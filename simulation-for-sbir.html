Simulation as preliminary data: evidence before you have money.

Updated July 28, 2026 · Free educational guide · verify details at the official sources linked below

SBIR reviewers reward preliminary evidence, and for hardware concepts the cheapest legitimate form is computational modeling: a CFD study, a stress or thermal analysis, an antenna pattern. A pre-funding team can produce it in weeks. Free tools carry real limits — student licenses prohibit commercial use — but evaluation licenses for commercial tools like Ansys exist precisely for this stage.

The evidence problem every pre-funding team has

Phase I reviewers score feasibility, and nothing argues feasibility like data. But a pre-funding hardware team can't afford a test campaign — that's why they're applying. The escape from this loop is analysis: a competently built simulation shows the physics closes, quantifies the risk you're proposing to retire, and demonstrates that your team can actually execute the work plan you're proposing. Reviewer guidance across agencies explicitly treats computational results as acceptable preliminary evidence.

What a two-week study can show

  • Aero/fluids (CFD): lift/drag or flow-rate curves across the operating envelope; pressure drops; mixing behavior.
  • Structures (FEA): stress margins against yield and fatigue at the worst load case; mass sensitivity.
  • Thermal: a heat budget proving the electronics survive the enclosure, or the TPS survives the trajectory.
  • Electromagnetics: antenna gain patterns, coupling, shielding effectiveness — before any range time.

One figure from a study like this, with honest assumptions stated, is worth pages of adjectives in a proposal.

After the award, the same tools carry you further

Phase I feasibility studies, Phase II design iteration, and — in regulated markets — formal V&V: FDA accepts credible computational modeling as evidence in device submissions, and defense programs expect analysis behind every qualification claim. For long-term affordability, eligible early-stage companies can also pursue the Ansys Startup Program (privately held, under 10 years old, under $5M revenue — verified case-by-case by Ansys and its partners).
