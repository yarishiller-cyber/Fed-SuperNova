Straight answers

Questions founders ask

We're pre-incorporation — two engineers with a CAD file and no company. Is any of this open to us?

Yes, and this is the right moment to plan. You can't hold a federal contract without an entity, but you can do the two things that matter most now: customer discovery (I-Corps-style) and early feasibility work. Many SBIR winners incorporated only once they had a target institute and a clear technical plan. Start with the need and the physics; the C-corp and SAM.gov registration come next.

Do these grants take equity in my startup?

The core programs here — NIH SBIR/STTR, ARPA-H, BARDA DRIVe, MTEC, NSF SBIR — are non-dilutive. They fund the work without taking ownership. Some later-stage tracks (like certain NIH Phase IIB bridges) ask you to line up matching third-party investment, but the federal money itself isn't equity. Nothing on this site is a promise of funding; every program is awarded at the agency's sole discretion under its own rules.

Does the FDA really require simulation now?

It doesn't mandate simulation for every device, but as of the November 2023 CDRH guidance it has a formal framework — built on the ASME V&V 40 standard — for trusting computational modeling as evidence in a submission. In practice, for physics-heavy devices, a credible model is increasingly expected rather than optional, and the FDA has programs (MDDT, in-silico trials) specifically designed to reward it. See our V&V40 article.

Which simulation tool should I use, and can I try it before committing?

The right tool depends on your device's dominant physics — structural (Ansys Mechanical, LS-DYNA), fluids (Fluent), electromagnetics (HFSS, Maxwell), or electronics reliability (Icepak, Sherlock). We can help you arrange an Ansys evaluation license, live engineering support, and free learning content through a channel partner so you can test the fit before you build it into a proposal. Evals and the Ansys Startup Program are Ansys programs offered at Ansys and partner sole discretion, and you're always free to use any partner you like.

Can simulation actually reduce my clinical trial size?

Sometimes — that's the promise of in-silico trials. A validated model run across a virtual-patient cohort can supplement clinical evidence, cover rare anatomies, and in some cases reduce the number of enrolled patients needed for statistical power. It rarely replaces a trial outright today, but it can shrink cost and timeline. The key word is validated: the credibility has to hold up under the FDA framework first.
