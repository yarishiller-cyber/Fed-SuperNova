How to write a winning RPP white paper

Updated July 29, 2026 · Free educational guide · verify details at the official sources below

In most OTA competitions you submit a short white paper first; only the strongest are invited to a full proposal. Winning ones are specific about the problem, concrete about the approach, honest about risk, and — critically — show feasibility with real evidence rather than adjectives.

The white paper is the gate

Consortia use a two-step funnel: a short white paper (against an RPP or RFS), then an invited full proposal. Most teams are filtered at the white paper. It's usually just a few pages, which is exactly why it's hard — you have no room to hide.

What reviewers are actually scoring

Four things, in plain terms: Do you understand their problem (not a generic version)? Is your approach technically sound and specific? Can you actually execute — team, schedule, cost? And is there a transition path — will this matter to a program after the prototype?

Structure that works

Lead with the problem in the government's own words. State your solution in one crisp paragraph. Then feasibility — this is where you win or lose. Then a milestone plan with realistic cost and schedule. Then your team and why you specifically. Close with the transition story.

If your project needs simulation

The single fastest way to lift a white paper from 'interesting' to 'executable' is one credible simulation result. An Ansys eval through a partner can produce exactly that figure.

Show feasibility, don't assert it

"Innovative," "revolutionary" and "cutting-edge" score nothing. A thermal map showing your electronics stay in spec, a stress plot proving the structure survives, an antenna pattern hitting the gain target — that is feasibility. Reviewers read hundreds of white papers; the one with a real analysis figure reads as executable while the rest read as hopeful. See simulation for OTA prototypes for what to model.

Common ways to lose

Ignoring the stated evaluation criteria. Being vague to sound broad. A schedule no one believes. No transition story. And submitting to a consortium you haven't joined — you can't bid until you're a member (see membership mechanics).

Official sources: S2MARTS — Requests for Solutions · MITRE AiDA. Figures change; confirm on the official page before relying on them.
