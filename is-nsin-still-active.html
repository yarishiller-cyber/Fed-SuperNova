Is NSIN still active after joining DIU?

Updated July 29, 2026 · Free educational guide · verify details at the official sources below

Yes — NSIN was absorbed into the Defense Innovation Unit in fall 2024, but its programming continues under DIU. Vector (accelerator), Propel (commercial-accelerator collaborations), Hacks, and Bootcamps still run, now tied more tightly to DIU's OnRamp Hubs. The brand persists; the parent org changed.

The short answer

Yes, NSIN still exists — but it's now a program office nested inside the Defense Innovation Unit rather than a standalone organization. The consolidation happened in fall 2024, part of a broader move to unify DoD's commercial-tech front doors under DIU.

What actually changed

Organizationally, NSIN reports up through DIU. Practically, that means tighter integration with DIU's OnRamp Hubs and the broader CSO pipeline. The programs themselves — the reason founders cared about NSIN — kept running.

What survived

The flagship dual-use programs continue. Vector is the startup accelerator that pairs teams with OnRamp Hubs and helps them carve pathways toward fielding capability. Propel collaborates with commercial accelerators; its portfolio ventures have historically pulled in roughly $100M+ in DoD funding and $250M in private investment. The Hacks (problem-focused hackathons) and Bootcamps (founder education) also continue.

If your project needs simulation

NSIN programs are on-ramps, not the build itself — when your dual-use concept firms up, simulation is the next step. Ask about an Ansys evaluation to turn a Vector idea into a testable design.

How to engage now

Watch both the NSIN site and DIU channels for open cohorts and events. If you're outside a coastal tech hub, your nearest OnRamp Hub is the natural entry point — mentoring, showcases, and warm introductions into the ecosystem. Cohort applications open on their own cadence, so getting on the mailing list early beats scrambling once a window is already half over.

Why the merger happened

The consolidation wasn't a demotion — it reflects a deliberate strategy to make DoD's commercial-tech front door less confusing. For years, founders faced an alphabet soup of overlapping programs. Folding NSIN into DIU put the education-and-community programs (NSIN) under the same roof as the money-and-transition machinery (CSOs, OTs, NSIC), so a founder can move from a hackathon to a funded prototype without switching organizations. Expect the branding to keep evolving as that integration matures.

Who each program suits

Hacks and Bootcamps are for the earliest teams — students, researchers, and founders testing whether they even have a defense angle. Vector suits a formed startup ready to build a network and a fielding pathway. Propel suits a venture ready for accelerator-style support and follow-on capital. None of them write you a large check directly; that's what the CSO and NSIC are for.

Where it leads

Think of NSIN programming as the on-ramp and the DIU Commercial Solutions Opening as the highway. Vector and Propel help you understand the mission and build a network; the CSO and NSIC are where non-dilutive dollars actually change hands. Used together, they form a coherent path from idea to prototype.
