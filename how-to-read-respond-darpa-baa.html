How to read and respond to a DARPA BAA

Updated July 29, 2026 · Free educational guide · verify details at the official sources below

A DARPA BAA is a program's public solicitation. The smart path is abstract-first: submit a short abstract, and only write a full proposal if the program manager encourages it. Read the BAA's technical metrics and evaluation criteria carefully — they tell you exactly what you must prove.

What a BAA actually is

A Broad Agency Announcement is the document a DARPA program uses to solicit proposals. It is not a grant form — it's a detailed statement of the hard problem, the quantitative metrics you must hit, the schedule, the funding structure, and exactly how proposals will be evaluated. Most are posted on SAM.gov and linked from the DARPA opportunities page.

Read the metrics first, not the vision

Skip past the inspiring framing and find the table of technical objectives and metrics. That table is the whole game: it tells you the specific number your technology must achieve. If you can't credibly draw a path to those numbers, no amount of polish saves the proposal. Then read the evaluation criteria and their relative weights — they tell you where to spend your effort.

Abstract first, always

DARPA strongly encourages submitting an abstract (a short summary) before a full proposal. Do this. An abstract costs you days, not weeks, and the PM's response tells you whether a full proposal is worth writing. A discouraging response is a gift — it just saved you a month.

If your project needs simulation

The technical volume of a BAA response is where credible simulation results earn their keep — an Ansys evaluation license through a channel partner can help you produce them before deadline.

Engage the program manager

During the open period you can ask technical questions, and at proposers days you can hear the PM explain what they really want. Program managers are people betting their reputation on a portfolio; they want to find performers who will succeed. Show up informed, respect their time, and ask sharp questions.

What a winning technical volume contains

The strongest responses do three things: they state a clear, testable hypothesis; they present a credible technical approach with real evidence (preliminary data, prototypes, and simulation results) that the physics works; and they lay out a de-risking plan with honest risks and mitigations. Vague confidence loses. Quantified, evidence-backed claims win.

Next steps

If a full program feels too big for where you are, look at fast, small Disruption Opportunities and seedlings to prove feasibility first, and read how DARPA selects performers so your response answers the questions evaluators are actually asking.

Official sources: DARPA — opportunities · SAM.gov. Figures change; confirm on the official page before relying on them.
