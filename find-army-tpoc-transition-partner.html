Transition

How to Find Your Army Transition Partner (the TPOC)

Updated July 29, 2026 · Free educational guide · verify details at the official sources below

A TPOC (Technical Point of Contact) is the government engineer or program lead who owns the requirement behind your SBIR topic and champions your technology toward a program of record. You find one by identifying the DEVCOM center or PEO that authored your topic, engaging during the pre-release question window, and building the relationship through Phase I performance.

Non-dilutive prize and SBIR money feels like the finish line. It is not. It is a bridge to a real Army buyer, and the single most important person on the far side is your TPOC — the Technical Point of Contact. Companies that transition well treat winning the TPOC as the actual goal; the award is just how you earn their attention.

Who the TPOC is

The TPOC is the government engineer or program lead who owns the requirement your SBIR topic addresses. They often authored the topic. They know which program office needs the capability, what the acceptance criteria are, and whether your Phase I turns into Phase II and eventually a program of record. No engaged TPOC, no transition — the technology stalls in the “valley of death” between R&D and fielding.

Step 1 — Trace the topic to its owner

Every Army SBIR topic maps to an originating organization — usually a DEVCOM center or a Program Executive Office (PEO). The topic text and its keywords tell you the domain. That organization is where your TPOC lives. If you came in through an xTech competition, the SME panel that judged your pitch often includes the very people who become your technical contacts.

If your project needs simulation

Timing rule: you can ask a TPOC questions during a topic's pre-release window, but once the solicitation opens the Army bars direct technical conversations with topic authors. Do your relationship-building early.

Step 2 — Engage in the pre-release window

Army topics pre-release before the submission window opens — new topics commonly appear on the first Wednesday of each month during the fiscal year. During pre-release you may contact the topic author with questions. Once the solicitation formally opens, the Army bars direct technical conversations with topic authors to keep the competition fair. So the pre-release window is your one clean chance to introduce yourself, clarify the real need, and signal that you understand the problem.

Step 3 — Earn the relationship through delivery

After award, treat the TPOC as a design partner, not a grant officer. Deliver early, share honest results including what failed, and translate your progress into their mission language. A TPOC who trusts you writes the Phase II justification, introduces you to the program office holding the budget, and advocates when transition dollars are scarce.

If you don't have one yet

Meet future TPOCs where they gather: DEVCOM industry days, AUSA events, and xTech pitch rounds. Read open topics on the Army SBIR site to learn which offices are actively seeking your kind of technology, then align your proposal to a named user. The best proposals name the transition partner explicitly — it tells evaluators the money will not die in the lab.

Official sources: Army SBIR|STTR FAQ & Resources · Army SBIR|STTR Program. Figures change; confirm on the official page before relying on them.
