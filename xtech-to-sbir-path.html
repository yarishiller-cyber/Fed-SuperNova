How to Apply

The xTech-to-SBIR path: from a prize check to a $2M contract

Updated July 29, 2026 · Free educational guide · verify details at the official sources below

xTech winners earn an exclusive invitation to submit an Army SBIR proposal without competing in the open topic pool. Mature technologies can skip Phase I entirely via Direct-to-Phase-II, going straight to a prototyping contract worth up to ~$2M.

Two funding worlds, one bridge

xTech pays prizes. Army SBIR pays contracts. The bridge between them is the exclusive submission lane that xTech winners receive — you get invited to submit an SBIR proposal instead of fighting through the full open pool.

Phase I: prove it's feasible

Army SBIR Phase I is a short feasibility contract, typically 3–6 months and up to ~$250K. Army topics are increasingly invitation-style and tied to a real transition partner — a program office that actually wants the capability. That's the difference between SBIR that leads somewhere and SBIR that ends in a report on a shelf.

Direct-to-Phase-II: skip the line

If you've already done Phase-I-equivalent work with other money — which many xTech winners have — you can pursue Direct-to-Phase-II. You submit feasibility documentation plus a Phase II proposal and go straight to a prototyping contract worth up to ~$2M, no separate Phase I award required.

If your project needs simulation

A validated Ansys model is often the strongest evidence in a Phase I technical volume — we can arrange an evaluation license and engineering support to build one.

What a strong proposal shows

Army reviewers want to see risk retired, not risk described. That's where engineering evidence matters: a structural, thermal, aerodynamic or electromagnetic analysis that shows you already understand the failure modes and have a design that survives them. For the physics behind that, see simulation for Army ground and soldier systems.

The timeline mindset

Winners typically submit SBIR proposals within weeks of a competition's final selection — the xTech|Inversion round, for instance, names winners in September and invites Phase I proposals right after. Have your technical story, team, and transition narrative ready before you win, not after.

The honest caveat

None of this is guaranteed. The lane is an invitation to submit, not a promise of an award, and every contract is competitive and at the Army's discretion. Treat the prize as validation and a head start — not a signed deal.
