Every major DoD OTA consortium, and what it costs to join

Updated July 29, 2026 · Free educational guide · verify details at the official sources below

Most DoD OTA money flows through member-only consortia. Fees are low — from free (DIBC) to about $250–$1,000 a year (NSTXL, SOSSEC, TReX, MCDC) plus MTEC's 2% assessment on funded awards. One membership usually unlocks a whole family of OTAs, so the real question is which consortium matches your technology.

Why this list is hard to find

There's no official, neutral directory of DoD OTA consortia with fees in one place — the closest is MITRE's AiDA. So here's a plain, comparison-first map. Pick by technology fit first, fee second.

The microelectronics & space stack — NSTXL

S2MARTS (trusted microelectronics, RF, strategic/spectrum missions, out of NSWC Crane) and SpEC (the Space Enterprise Consortium) are both run by NSTXL. One NSTXL membership — tiered ~$250 to ~$10,000/yr by entity type and revenue — covers its consortia, and there's a free S2MARTS Research tier for research-only work. S2MARTS solicits via Requests for Solutions (RFS); SpEC via Requests for Prototype Proposals (RPPs).

Military medicine — MTEC

MTEC runs the USAMRDC medical OTA. Dues are ~$1,000 (small business/academia/non-profit) or ~$5,000 (large business), plus a 2% assessment on funded awards. Awards have ranged ~$50K to $25M.

C4ISR, info warfare & armaments — the ATI/SOSSEC family

SOSSEC manages eight-plus OTAs (C4ISR, GEOINT, sensors) for one ~$500/yr fee. Advanced Technology International (ATI) runs several more: IWRP 3 (Navy information warfare, open membership), TReX II (Army modeling & simulation, ~$500/yr, US-only + DD2345), the National Armaments Consortium (munitions/energetics), and MCDC (medical countermeasures, ~$500/yr).

Industrial base — DIBC (free)

DIBC feeds DPA Title III and IBAS industrial-base funding, accepts unsolicited white papers, and is free to join today (dues capped at ~$250 if ever added).

How to actually join

Every one needs the same basics: a CAGE code, a UEI (get both via SAM.gov), and a signed membership agreement; armaments and training OTAs also need a DD2345. See how consortium membership works for the step-by-step.

Official sources: MITRE AiDA — existing OT consortia · NSTXL membership FAQ · MTEC. Figures change; confirm on the official page before relying on them.
