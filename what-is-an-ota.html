What is an OTA, and why do startups love it?

Updated July 29, 2026 · Free educational guide · verify details at the official sources below

An Other Transaction (OT) is a contract the DoD signs under Other Transaction Authority — a legal path that sits outside most Federal Acquisition Regulation (FAR) rules. It lets the government fund prototypes fast, with flexible IP terms and lighter overhead, and it deliberately favors non-traditional contractors like startups.

The short version

Other Transactions are how the Department of Defense buys prototypes when it wants speed and access to companies that don't normally do government work. They're authorized by statute (10 U.S.C. 4021 and 4022), and they sit outside most of the Federal Acquisition Regulation. No cascade of FAR/DFARS clauses. Negotiable intellectual-property terms. Milestone-based payments instead of cost-reimbursement bureaucracy.

Why the model exists

Congress built OTA because the traditional acquisition system was too slow and too alien for the commercial technology base — the exact companies the DoD now needs most. An OT can be negotiated and awarded in weeks or months, not the year-plus a big FAR contract often takes.

Why founders love it

Three reasons. First, it's non-dilutive — the government funds your prototype and takes no equity. Second, the IP terms are negotiable, so you can often keep commercial rights to what you build. Third, there's a real path to production: a successful prototype OT can convert to a production OT without re-competing.

The catch

OTA isn't a grant. You're delivering a prototype against milestones, and the government expects results. It's also not usually a direct-to-you affair — most defense OTAs are run through a consortium, and you have to be a member to bid. That's what the rest of this site is about: which consortium to join, how membership works, and how to write the white paper that wins.

How it compares

If you're weighing OTA against SBIR grants or a standard FAR contract, read OTA vs FAR vs SBIR — they solve different problems and the best-funded startups use more than one.
