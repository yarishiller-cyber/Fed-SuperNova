APFIT explained — and why you can't apply directly

Updated July 29, 2026 · Free educational guide · verify details at the official sources below

APFIT (Accelerate the Procurement and Fielding of Innovative Technologies) is a DoD program that buys production quantities of already-proven small-business technology to push it across the valley of death. Companies cannot apply directly — a DoD program office or component must nominate the project. FY26 awards averaged over $30M, up to a $50M statutory cap, and the program passed $2B in cumulative awards.

What APFIT actually does

APFIT — the Accelerate the Procurement and Fielding of Innovative Technologies program, run out of OUSD(R&E) and A&S — exists to solve one specific problem: a small company proves a great capability with SBIR or a prototype OTA, and then it dies because there's no money to buy it at scale before a program of record catches up. APFIT is the bridge. It's not an R&D grant. It's procurement: the government buys production units of your already-mature tech and fields them.

The numbers are serious. FY26 awards averaged more than $30M each, the largest single award hit roughly $49.7M against a $50M statutory cap, and the program crossed $2 billion in cumulative awards — with its first-ever software capabilities funded in the final FY26 round.

The rule nobody puts in the headline

Here's what trips people up: you cannot apply to APFIT. There is no open portal where a founder submits a proposal. Instead, a DoD program office or component has to nominate your project. Nominations flow up through the components, get evaluated, and a subset get funded.

So the real game isn't writing a perfect application — it's winning an internal sponsor. You need a program office that wants your capability enough to spend their nomination slot on you. That means fielding a demonstrated product (roughly TRL 8+), having a clear end user, and making it absurdly easy for that sponsor to say yes.

How to get nominated

Practically, that looks like: finish a strong SBIR Phase II or prototype OTA; build a relationship with the program office that would actually use your product; give them the transition story, the unit economics, and the production plan; and time your pitch to the component's nomination cycle. Events like the DIB Accelerator and service industry days exist precisely so you can meet those sponsors.

Where it sits in the funding stack

APFIT is non-dilutive and it buys product — but it doesn't build your factory. That's where the DoD Office of Strategic Capital loans and DPA Title III come in. Read crossing the valley of death for how these fit together.

Official sources: APFIT program office (ac.cto.mil) · War Department: final FY26 APFIT awards. Figures change; confirm on the official page before relying on them.
