DOE clean-energy funding after the 2025 reorg

Updated July 29, 2026 · Free educational guide · verify details at the official sources below

In November 2025 DOE reorganized: EERE ceased to exist as a standalone office and its functions moved into the new Office of Critical Minerals and Energy Innovation (CMEI), organized into three pillars. ARPA-E remains its own agency, DOE SBIR/STTR continues through the Office of Science, and lab fellowships and American-Made prizes still run — the names and web pages moved, the money largely didn't disappear.

What actually happened

In November 2025, DOE announced a major realignment. The Office of Energy Efficiency and Renewable Energy — EERE, the home of a lot of clean-energy funding — no longer exists as a standalone office. Its functions were folded, along with several other offices, into a new Office of Critical Minerals and Energy Innovation (CMEI) that reports directly to the Energy Secretary.

The new structure

CMEI is organized into three pillars: the Office of Critical Minerals, Materials and Manufacturing (CM3); the Office of Energy Technology (E-Tech), which carries much of the R&D mission; and the Office of Innovation, Affordability and Consumer Choice (IACC). If you're hunting for a program that used to live under EERE, it's almost certainly under one of these now — the URL changed, not necessarily the program.

What did NOT change

Three things founders care about stayed put. ARPA-E is a separate agency and was not merged into CMEI — it runs as before. DOE SBIR/STTR continues through the Office of Science with its usual cycles. And the lab-embedded fellowships and American-Made prizes still operate, though some now sit under CMEI's umbrella.

What to do about it

Two practical moves. First, when a bookmarked DOE funding page 404s, search the program name plus "CMEI" or check eXCHANGE and the Office of Science SBIR site directly — the content usually moved, not vanished. Second, don't let the org-chart churn stall your technical work. The physics of your hardware is the same no matter which office signs the award, and a fundable technology is a fundable technology.
