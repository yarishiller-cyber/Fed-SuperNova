AFWERX Pitch Day: what actually happens, and how to walk out with a contract

Updated July 29, 2026 · Free educational guide · verify details at the official sources below

AFWERX Pitch Day is a short, high-tempo evaluation where selected small businesses present a dual-use technology to Air or Space Force evaluators. Winners can receive same-day Phase I or Direct-to-Phase-II award decisions. You get there by submitting through the DSIP portal against an open solicitation, clearing the paper review, then delivering a tight, customer-anchored pitch that proves military relevance and technical feasibility.

"Pitch day" is one of the most searched phrases in the AFWERX world, and also one of the most misunderstood. It is not a demo day for VCs. It is a government source-selection event compressed into minutes, where Air Force and Space Force evaluators decide whether to put a Small Business Innovation Research (SBIR) contract behind your technology.

How the day is structured

You do not get to a pitch day by showing up. You submit a proposal through the DSIP portal against an open AFWERX solicitation, clear the written review, and are then invited to present. On the day itself you get a short slot — often well under fifteen minutes including questions — in front of a panel that typically pairs a contracting officer with technical and end-user representatives.

The tempo is deliberate. Evaluators may see dozens of companies, and the best awards decisions can land the same day or within days. That speed is the whole point of the AFWERX model, so respect it: an over-built, jargon-heavy pitch dies fast.

What the judges are really scoring

Three things decide your outcome. First, military relevance: can you name a real Department of the Air Force problem and, ideally, a real customer who feels it? Second, technical feasibility: is the phase of work you are proposing actually achievable with the money and time on offer? Third, dual-use commercial merit: is there a market beyond the government that de-risks the bet?

If your project needs simulation

Judges reward founders who show, not tell. A short simulation clip — your design flying, thermal loads resolving, a structure surviving vibe — lands harder than a bullet list. A prep checklist that works

  • Open with the customer, not the company. Lead with the mission gap and who owns it.
  • Know your award path. Be clear whether you are pitching a Phase I feasibility study or a Direct-to-Phase-II (D2P2) with prior work to point to.
  • Bring proof. Test data, a working prototype, or a simulation result beats a roadmap slide.
  • Rehearse the money. If asked how you would spend a Phase I budget, answer crisply — see our note on the fee and indirect rates in the sibling guides.
  • Have a next step ready. The strongest teams already understand the STRATFI and TACFI matching path that follows a strong Phase II.

After the pitch

An award is the start, not the finish. Phase I proves feasibility; Phase II builds it; and the real prize is a customer who wants to keep buying.
