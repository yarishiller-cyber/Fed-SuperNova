DARPA's Embedded Entrepreneurship Initiative (EEI)

Updated July 29, 2026 · Free educational guide · verify details at the official sources below

EEI gives current DARPA performers roughly $250K–$340K in non-dilutive funding to hire an embedded entrepreneur and build a real commercialization strategy for defense and commercial markets. In its pilot, EEI helped about 30 pre-seed teams raise over $100M in private investment.

The gap EEI fills

DARPA is good at funding hard research and historically less focused on what happens after — turning a working prototype into a company customers can buy from. The Embedded Entrepreneurship Initiative was built to close that gap by embedding business talent inside DARPA-funded research teams.

What you actually get

Selected teams receive roughly $250K–$340K in non-dilutive funding, structured to hire a seasoned entrepreneur or business executive who works alongside the technical team for one to two years. That person builds a genuine go-to-market strategy spanning defense and commercial markets, and DARPA adds mentorship plus investor and corporate connections through its Commercial Strategy Office.

The results so far

The numbers are the pitch: over its pilot, EEI helped about 30 pre-seed research teams raise more than $100M in U.S. private investment. Recent cohorts continue to select teams — for example, an Iowa State spinout was selected in 2026 with ~$340K to commercialize a waste-to-fuel process. The point is that a small amount of the right non-dilutive money, plus an embedded operator, can unlock far larger private rounds.

If your project needs simulation

Investors evaluating a deep-tech team want to see the physics is real; validated simulation models are a durable asset you can put in a data room, and an Ansys eval through a partner helps you build them.

Who qualifies

EEI is aimed at existing DARPA-funded research teams with real commercialization potential — not the general public. If you're already a performer, this is a program to raise with your program manager. If you're not yet funded by DARPA, EEI is a reason to treat commercialization as part of your story from day one, because it becomes available downstream.

How to pursue it

Talk to your program manager and the Commercial Strategy Office; selection weighs your commercialization strategy and the strength of your team. If you're still upstream of a DARPA award, start with how DARPA funds deep-tech and the seedling path to become a performer in the first place.

Official sources: DARPA — EEI · DARPA — Commercial Strategy Office. Figures change; confirm on the official page before relying on them.
